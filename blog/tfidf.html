TF-IDF Explained: The Simple Idea Behind My Movie Recommendations

One of the most useful things I learned from MovieGPT is that a recommendation engine does not have to start with a neural network.

The problem

A movie description is written for humans. A computer needs numbers if we want to compare thousands of descriptions mathematically. TF-IDF is one way to make that conversion.

TF: Term Frequency

TF asks: how often does this word appear in this document? If “space” appears several times in a movie description, it becomes more important to that description.

IDF: Inverse Document Frequency

IDF adds context. A word that appears in almost every document is not very useful for distinguishing titles. A word that appears in only a small number of documents can be much more informative.

TF-IDF combines those ideas. Common words receive less influence, while words that help identify a particular document receive more.

From words to vectors

After vectorization, each title becomes a numerical vector. Imagine every movie becoming a point on a map with thousands of dimensions. Similar content should occupy nearby areas.

Why cosine distance?

MovieGPT uses cosine distance with NearestNeighbors. Rather than focusing on the absolute length of two vectors, cosine distance compares their direction. This is useful for text because we often care more about the pattern of terms than the raw number of words.

Simple mental model: TF-IDF turns text into coordinates; nearest-neighbor search asks which coordinates are closest.

Its limitations

TF-IDF does not truly understand meaning. “Car” and “automobile” can be treated as different words, even though a person knows they are related. It also depends heavily on the quality of the text fields we feed into it.

Why I would still use it

Not every problem needs a large model. If a simple, explainable algorithm solves the core task, it can be the better engineering choice. MovieGPT taught me that good AI systems are often combinations of different techniques rather than one magic model.
